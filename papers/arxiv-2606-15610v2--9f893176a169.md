###### Abstract

Passing an exact-clone null gate does not entail passing a matched
target-selection utility gate. We call a non-tie preference between
exact duplicates dark current. On 102 prespecified fresh RewardBench 2
rows, all four configuration–criterion cells report 0/102 clone
preferences. Both neutral utility arms pass, but strict utility lower
bounds of .539 and .745 miss the .75 working threshold. Conversely,
re-evaluation of the same rows on two additional configurations yields a
neutral gpt-oss utility pass with 2/102 clone preferences, leaving
low-null behavior uncertified. A separately frozen 485-row extension
passes both aggregate working gates under both criteria on one local
raw-text configuration, yet strict Precise Instruction Following
(Precise IF) alignment is 58/154 (.377). Thus matched null and utility
results can diverge, and aggregate passage can coexist with weak subset
alignment. Interpretation is conditional on the declared configurations,
fixed source mixtures, source-label targets, and study-specific working
models. The bounds are marginal, the subset rate is descriptive, and
non-certification does not prove a defect.

## 1 Introduction

LLM-as-a-judge systems have become evaluation infrastructure. MT-Bench
and Chatbot Arena demonstrated their practical scale, while also
documenting sensitivity to presentation and response attributes (Zheng
et al., 2023). Subsequent work has measured position bias (Shi et al.,
2025), multidimensional bias (Ye et al., 2025), benchmark-level
evaluator competence (Wang et al., 2025), and latent reliability with
item-response theory (IRT) (Choi et al., 2026). A reported score
therefore characterizes a measurement pipeline, not only the evaluated
model.

This distinction matters when the same judge supports scientific claims,
model selection, or best-of-$`N`$ search. High aggregate agreement can
coexist with poor decisions because the deployment distribution and
error costs differ from the validation benchmark (Landesberg & Narayan,
2025; Landesberg, 2026). Null models can exploit evaluator regularities
to obtain high apparent win rates (Zheng et al., 2025). Disagreement is
not always judge error either: some pairs admit multiple defensible
ratings (Guerdan et al., 2025). An audit must separate absence of
signal, nuisance sensitivity, target sensitivity, abstention, and
downstream loss.

Here, _dark current_ denotes a non-tie response to two exactly identical
inputs. The term is an operational analogy, not a physical model of an
LLM. Exact-clone behavior must also be stratified. A nonblank text
duplicate probes judging on ordinary text, whereas empty and
whitespace-only inputs probe pipeline edge handling. Pooling these
strata can make an edge-case failure appear to be a general property of
the judge.

The audit asks three questions: can the evaluation client produce
eligible verdicts, does it invent a preference between exact duplicates,
and does it select the declared target on signal pairs? These questions
require separate evidence. The primary 102-row RewardBench 2 study
supplies a null-passing, utility-missing case. Re-evaluation on
additional configurations passes utility without meeting the low-null
evidence criterion. The nonoverlapping 485-row extension adds a third
lesson: an aggregate pass can conceal weak source-label alignment within
a prespecified source subset.

The protocol combines input-stratified null tests, order-canonical
response patterns, and separate operational and utility gates. Declared
application losses give these measurements a decision context; their use
has not been tested as a deployment intervention. Table 1 identifies the
configurations across studies. Benchmark labels and finite-domain code
certificates define different targets; the working statistical rules are
interpreted in Section 3.5, and their scope is consolidated in
Section 10. The purpose is to prevent unsupported adoption decisions,
not to rank model families.

## 2 Related work

#### Judge competence and bias.

MT-Bench established LLM judging as a scalable evaluation method (Zheng
et al., 2023). Evaluator-capability benchmarks (Wang et al., 2025) and
multidimensional bias audits (Ye et al., 2025) address complementary
questions; Judge Reliability Harness (JRH) stress-tests judgment
accuracy under formatting, paraphrase, verbosity, and label-flipping
perturbations (Dev et al., 2026); and Shi et al. measure repetition
stability, position consistency, and preference fairness (Shi et al.,
2025). These methods estimate different quantities. Our order-canonical
accounting separates stable content preference, same-slot preference,
and one-sided commits through an exact identity; it is not a new
position-bias mechanism. IRT and conformal/transitivity diagnostics
expose item-level reliability and criterion dependence (Choi et al.,
2026; Gupta & Kumar, 2026), while signal-detection analyses distinguish
sensitivity from decision criterion (Cacioli, 2026).

#### Null response and indeterminate ratings.

MT-Bench reports ties for identical answers as a calibration check
(Zheng et al., 2023, Section 3.3). We turn that check into a declared,
input-stratified null estimand and assess target utility separately.
Norman et al. document that high test–retest stability can coexist with
position bias and recommend combined validation (Norman et al., 2026).
Our audit focuses on exact-clone null behavior, target selection, and
operational eligibility under study-specific working rules.

Null-model attacks show that benchmark regularities can produce high win
rates without corresponding generation quality (Zheng et al., 2025).
Rating-indeterminacy work distinguishes evaluator error from legitimate
ambiguity in the target label (Guerdan et al., 2025). Exact clones
provide a null with zero content contrast within a candidate pair; this
differs from repeating a request to assess stability. We assess utility
separately against source labels or finite-domain truth under
study-specific validity and statistical rules. For natural-language
distinct answers, equivalence false preference ($`\mathrm{EFP}`$) is
deferred until semantic equality has been independently validated.
Finite-code equivalence is separately certified on explicitly declared
domains.

#### Causal and decision validity.

Causal Judge Evaluation evaluates a surrogate under interventions
relevant to a target estimand (Landesberg & Narayan, 2025);
best-of-$`N`$ analyses show that good average scores need not preserve
selection decisions (Landesberg, 2026). We therefore report a loss
function over clone errors, wrong choices, ties, and invalid outputs.
The benchmark risk surface excludes the unvalidated natural-language
equivalence term. The finite-code study reports its certified
equivalence null separately, without substituting it for human semantic
equivalence. This connects each reported axis to a declared use
distribution and loss function.

#### Documentation as measurement.

Datasheets and model cards make provenance, intended use, and subgroup
behavior inspectable (Gebru et al., 2021; Mitchell et al., 2019). We
adapt that documentation logic to the evaluator itself, but require each
reported axis to be linked to a falsifiable stress test and a declared
downstream loss.

Recent work also studies judgment elicitation and downstream use jointly
(Zhang et al., 2026). Utility auditing alone is therefore not a
sufficient novelty claim. The present protocol instead fixes matched
null and signal units, declares the input strata and configuration, and
separates the operational validity of a result from useful selection
behavior.

## 3 Protocol and estimands

### 3.1 Judge configurations and outcomes

A judge configuration is the tuple

```math
J=(\text{checkpoint, chat template, rubric, decoder, parser, serving backend}).
```

Changing any component creates a new configuration; parameter count or
model family alone does not define a judge. For task $`i`$, pair type
$`s`$, presentation order $`o\in\{ab,ba\}`$, and criterion prompt $`c`$,
the parser returns $`Y_{isoc}\in\{1,2,\mathrm{tie},\mathrm{invalid}\}`$.
We map slot labels to a canonical content outcome
$`W_{isoc}\in\{u_{i},v_{i},\mathrm{tie},\mathrm{invalid}\}`$. All order
analyses use $`W`$, never raw slot labels. In the results, AB and BA
denote A-first and B-first presentation.

once under a prespecified blocked design. Even with stateless calls and
temperature zero, factor-specific causal interpretation still requires
stable serving, no interference, and adequate carryover. Randomization
tests concern fixed items and named configurations; they do not identify
model-family or task-population effects.

### 3.2 Null strata: exact clones are not edge inputs

Let $`N=\mathbb{I}\{Y\notin\{\mathrm{tie},\mathrm{invalid}\}\}`$. For
criterion $`c`$,

```math
\displaystyle\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{core}}(J,c) \displaystyle=\Pr(N=1\mid A=B,\ |\operatorname{strip}(A)|>0,c), \tag{1}
```

```math
\displaystyle\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{edge}}(J,c) \displaystyle=\Pr(N=1\mid A=B,\ A\in\{\emptyset,\mathrm{whitespace}\},c). \tag{2}
```

Here $`\operatorname{strip}(A)`$ removes leading and trailing
whitespace; its positive length excludes empty and whitespace-only
strings. The first is the primary exact-null estimand. The second is a
pipeline stress diagnostic and must be reported by input stratum; its
operational weight may be zero in a system that rejects empty candidates
before judging. A pooled value is meaningful only after the deployment
mixture over strata is declared.

#### Distinct-answer extension.

For nonidentical answers whose equality is supplied by a benchmark
construction or semantic audit, we define the separate estimand

```math
\mathrm{EFP}(J,c)=\Pr(N=1\mid A\sim B,\ A\neq B,c).
```

Exact identity guarantees zero content contrast; $`A\sim B`$ does not.
Because natural-language semantic equivalence was not independently
established, that arm is excluded from the benchmark evidence and its
prevalence is set to zero in the benchmark risk surface. The later
finite-code equivalence arm is a different, domain-certified estimand. A
future preregistered extension may add this stratum after outcome-blind
construct validation.

### 3.3 Order-canonical nuisance decomposition

For a canonical distinct pair $`z_{i}=\{u_{i},v_{i}\}`$, both orders are
evaluated. Stable cross-sensitivity is the pair rate at which the same
content wins twice,

```math
\mathrm{SCS}=\Pr(W_{iab}=W_{iba}\in\{u_{i},v_{i}\}),
```

whereas positional false preference is the pair rate at which the same
_slot_ wins twice,

```math
\mathrm{PFP}=\Pr(Y_{iab}=Y_{iba}\in\{1,2\}).
```

The call-level raw false-preference rate $`\mathrm{RFP}_{0}`$ also
contains one-sided commits, for which one order ties and the other does
not. For clean three-label outputs,

```math
\mathrm{RFP}_{0}=\mathrm{SCS}+\mathrm{PFP}+\tfrac{1}{2}\Pr(\text{one-sided commit}).
```

High same-quality false preference may therefore arise from a slot
effect rather than stable content sensitivity.

### 3.4 Target sensitivity and criterion–resolution identification

For a pair with a constructively known better answer $`y_{i}^{*}`$ and
quality contrast $`\delta>0`$, target sensitivity is

```math
S_{\delta}(J,c)=\Pr(W_{isoc}=y_{i}^{*}),
```

with ties counted as misses and wrong choices reported separately. The
controlled ladder adds requirements by a prefix chain, so the higher
level Pareto-dominates the lower level on the declared checklist. The
ladder serves only as a positive control for that target.

The criterion contrast in abstention is

```math
\tau_{C}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{i}[T_{is}(c_{\rm strict})-T_{is}(c_{\rm base})],\quad T=\mathbb{I}\{Y=\mathrm{tie}\}.
```

A stricter prompt can reduce false preference by moving a decision
threshold, by degrading representation, or both. “Resolution is
unchanged” therefore requires an equivalence test on a predeclared
sensitivity contrast or psychometric slope. Failure to reject a slope
difference is insufficient. The current controlled evidence has only two
prompt operating points and sparse strict-prompt signal levels; it does
not identify invariant resolution.

### 3.5 Inference and validity gates

The task is the primary sampling cluster. For the initial reused-item
study, we use paired within-task contrasts and a task-cluster percentile
bootstrap for rates and exploratory risk; canonical-pair clustering is a
diagnostic sensitivity analysis. These intervals quantify
prompt-sampling variation under the observed deterministic schedule;
they do not remove temporal confounding, and no randomization-inference
$`p`$-value is reported for that study. Calls, reversed presentations,
and repeated rendered prompts are never treated as independent sampling
units.

The operational core asks whether exact-clone false preference is below
.05 while positive-control sensitivity remains useful. A configuration
is marked HOLD (unevaluable under its operational gate) if invalid
output exceeds 1%, an expected response is missing or mismatched to its
scheduled item, or content labels cannot be reconciled across
presentation orders. In the fresh-item primary study, four one-sided
exact-binomial clone tests (two configurations $`\times`$ two criteria)
form a fixed Holm family; invalid clone output is adverse. Signal
success requires a valid source-chosen verdict; tie, wrong, and invalid
count as zero. A prespecified subset-stratified task bootstrap supplies
the one-sided 95% lower utility bound, and a bound below .75 blocks
useful-low-null interpretation. Conditional randomization tests with
fixed multiplicity families assess criterion, order/interaction, and
clone-criterion contrasts. Appendix B gives the resampling counts and
assignment scheme. These tests are against schedule-wide sharp nulls; a
targeted statistic does not by itself identify that factor. The initial
study’s conservative invalid-cell rule and all concrete risk-grid values
are disclosed as post-outcome exploratory choices.

#### Working-model interpretation.

The clone-tail tests and Clopper–Pearson bounds use an independent,
common-probability Bernoulli working model for item-level adversity
within each declared cell. Outcome-blind selection of a fixed battery
does not establish these stochastic assumptions or IID sampling from a
deployment population. The resulting nominal bounds and adjusted tests
are therefore model-conditional audit summaries, alongside the observed
counts, rather than validated population-risk guarantees. Utility
bootstrap percentiles describe resampling of task composition within the
declared empirical strata or template families; they do not add
design-based population coverage. Operational completeness and decoder
validity are separate requirements. The .05 clone and .75 utility
cutoffs are prespecified working rules, not universally calibrated
deployment losses.

## 5 Primary study on fresh items

The primary study uses a fixed snapshot of RewardBench 2 (Malik et al.,
2026); exact dataset provenance is reported in Appendix B. A
deterministic outcome-blind ranking selects 17 rows from each of
Factuality, Focus, Math, Precise IF, Safety, and Ties. These 102 rows do
not overlap either reused-item study by row or prompt identity. Within
each row, the same fixed chosen answer is (i) duplicated exactly for the
clone null and (ii) matched against a source-rejected answer for signal.
Before selection, eligible signal pairs must differ by at most 20 words
and 25% relative word count; selection then minimizes absolute word and
character differences without judge outcomes. Length matching reduces
one nuisance. Inference remains limited to eligible rows, and source
labels are not human-preference labels.

Each configuration receives 204 clone calls (102 tasks $`\times`$ two
criteria) and 408 signal calls (102 tasks $`\times`$ two criteria
$`\times`$ two orders). A single randomized assignment is fixed before
any judge call. Twelve temporal blocks separate matched clone and signal
exposures, with criterion and order sequences balanced within the
design. No item is replaced after output, and the three benchmark-based
studies are never pooled. The prespecified analysis assigns each
configuration–criterion cell a joint clone-and-utility label, then
reports criterion- and order-targeted randomization tests separately.
Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-OptiQ-4bit are named
configurations, not samples from a population. Because RewardBench 2 is
public, we cannot claim that it is absent from judge pretraining.

The 51 chosen–chosen pairs in the reused-item Ties arm are excluded
because their semantic equivalence was not independently validated. They
contribute no estimate or term to the current risk calculation;
Appendix B states the requirements for any future study of this stratum.

## 6 Independently Verified Finite-Domain Code Study

To extend the null test beyond text identity without substituting
assumed human labels, we constructed a separate code-correctness study.
The 51-item human semantic-equivalence set remains unlabeled; the code
experiment measures a different, explicitly decidable construct. Its
protocol, item set, request schedule, source hashes, and oracle receipt
were frozen locally before scientific outcome collection. This
outcome-blind commitment is not third-party preregistration.

#### Finite-domain truth.

Eight fixed templates with 12 parameter settings yield 96 items. Each
specifies a finite integer or integer-list domain and contains a
reference implementation, a syntactically different equivalent
implementation, and a faulty implementation. An independent
specification implementation exhaustively checks 51,960 input vectors
and 155,880 candidate–input pairs. Both correct implementations agree on
every declared input, while each faulty implementation has at least one
verified disagreement. These certificates do not establish equivalence
on arbitrary Python inputs or human preference.

#### Design and serving.

The completed design has three service-reported configurations:
qwen3.8-flash-next, openai/gpt-oss-120b, and nemotron-3-super. Weight
digests are unverified. Each configuration completed all 960 scheduled
calls: per item and criterion, one reference/reference clone, two
ordered reference/equivalent comparisons, and two ordered
reference/faulty comparisons. The neutral rubric selects the
implementation correct on more valid inputs; the strict rubric
additionally requires a material functional advantage and permits a tie
for insufficient evidence. Both explicitly exclude style, length, and
efficiency. The schedule seed is 2026100802. Temperature is zero, the
output ceiling is 2,048 tokens, and the archived request profiles use
low reasoning effort for gpt-oss and disable thinking for the other two
configurations.

#### Capture and operational validity.

One request is in flight at a time, with a durable intent record before
dispatch and no retries or replacement of failed observations. A valid
response is exactly one JSON object with the sole key winner and value
A, B, or tie. Duplicate keys, fences, additional keys, truncated output,
and empty content are invalid and retained in denominators. A
configuration’s operational gate requires all 960 terminal records,
matching served-model identity, no ambiguous request, and at most 1%
invalid responses. Partial coverage yields HOLD. A transport failure or
model mismatch stops the campaign rather than silently resampling.

#### Outcome definitions.

Clone adversity is a non-tie or invalid response. For functional
equivalence, a task is adverse if either AB or BA is non-tie or invalid.
Canonicalizing presentation order separates ties, stable content
preference, position conflict, one-sided commitment, and invalid output.
Signal utility is the mean of the two order-specific successes; the
reference must beat the verified faulty implementation. Wrong choices,
ties, and invalid responses score zero. All six configuration/criterion
cells are retained; no best-subset report substitutes for the full
design.

#### Working statistical gates.

The fixed six-cell clone family uses the exact lower binomial tail for
$`H_{0}:p\geq.05`$ and Holm adjustment at .05. One-sided 95%
Clopper–Pearson bounds are marginal, not simultaneous; zero out of 96
has an upper bound of approximately .0307 and does not certify zero
risk. These are nominal working-model quantities: IID sampling from a
general task population is not established for parameterizations of
eight fixed templates, so population coverage and family-wise
calibration are not asserted. Utility is resampled within each of the
eight 12-item families for 20,000 equally weighted stratified bootstrap
replicates. Its operational usefulness threshold is a one-sided fifth
percentile at least .75. Two-sided percentile intervals are descriptive
and add no second gate. Resampling is conditional on these fixed
templates. Equivalence rates, canonical classes, paired
strict-minus-neutral utility, and per-order successes are secondary
descriptive results. The primary benchmark observations are not pooled
with this code study.

## 9 Decision-specific risk accounting

Criterion choice depends on deployment loss. Let $`\pi_{c}`$ be the
deployment probability of an exact-clone comparison, and let
$`\lambda_{c},\lambda_{W},\lambda_{T},\lambda_{I}`$ denote losses for
clone false preference, a wrong signal choice, a tie on a signal pair,
and parser-invalid output. Because natural-language distinct-answer
equivalence was not validated for the benchmark study, its prevalence is
fixed at $`\pi_{e}=0`$ in this illustrative surface. Finite-code
equivalence rates are not inserted into this benchmark deployment
mixture. For configuration $`J`$ and criterion $`C`$, the resulting risk
is

```math
\begin{split}R^{(0)}_{J,C}={}&\pi_{c}\{\lambda_{c}\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{core}}+\lambda_{I}\Pr(\mathrm{invalid}\mid c)\}\\
&+(1-\pi_{c})\{\lambda_{W}\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid s)+\lambda_{T}\Pr(\mathrm{tie}\mid s)+\lambda_{I}\Pr(\mathrm{invalid}\mid s)\}.\end{split} \tag{3}
```

$`\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{edge}}`$ remains a separate pipeline gate unless
empty or whitespace candidates are valid deployment events; in that case
the deployment mixture must add their prevalence and loss explicitly.

An illustrative sensitivity grid for $`R^{(0)}_{J,C}`$ uses the
post-outcome choices: $`\pi_{c}\in\{0,.01,.05\}`$,
$`\lambda_{c}\in\{.5,1,2,5\}`$, $`\lambda_{W}=1`$,
$`\lambda_{T}\in\{.1,.25,.5,1\}`$, and $`\lambda_{I}\in\{1,5\}`$,
yielding 96 scenarios. The grid specifies alternative loss choices for
the observed benchmark rates. Applications may value ties differently in
scientific adjudication and best-of-$`N`$ selection (Landesberg, 2026).

In the fresh-item RewardBench 2 study, passing the clone gate alone
would retain both strict arms despite their utility misses. Equation 3
makes the decision consequence explicit: criterion choice depends on the
relative costs of wrong choices, ties, and invalid responses.

#### From audit to selection.

Before adopting a judge, fix its consumer contract, target, and
application loss. Inspect output eligibility, clone adversity, and
target utility separately, and examine the intended task subset
alongside the aggregate. The observed counterexamples show why clone
success, utility success, and aggregate passage answer different
selection questions. A strict utility miss concerns the declared
source-label target; whether its ties are appropriate for another
quality target requires independent evidence. When wrong selections cost
more than ties, a conservative criterion can be worth considering under
Equation 3; a compulsory-choice application can instead penalize ties
heavily. The audit has not been shown to reduce deployment loss.

## 10 Limitations and scope

The controlled study has 10 synthetic tasks, one decoding seed, and no
human rating distribution; its edge strata each repeat one rendered
prompt. The fresh-item primary study adds fresh rows but not a new
dataset, language, or domain. Its source-chosen label is the benchmark
construct, and the fixed length gate restricts inference to rows
admitting a close-length chosen–rejected pair. Zero observed clone false
preferences is not zero probability: the exact one-sided upper bound is
.029 per 102-task cell and .00616 per 485-task extension cell. These are
marginal bounds for their separate working models. RewardBench 2 is
public, so contamination cannot be excluded. The current risk surface
fixes $`\pi_{e}=0`$ and therefore does not characterize deployment
events involving distinct but putatively equivalent answers; more
generally, any risk equation is only as useful as its declared
prevalence and losses.

We do not infer family or scale effects from the named benchmark
configurations. Template, decoder, parser, and backend are part of each
configuration. For the API-served configuration, we could verify only
the service-reported model identifier, not continuity of the underlying
weights; unobserved backend changes therefore remain possible. The
prespecified schedule disperses order and time but cannot rule out
cache, concurrency, or response-history effects. Qwen2.5’s
interaction-targeted rejection cautions against a criterion-only
interpretation. All randomization-test rejections remain schedule-wide
sharp-null results unless stable serving, no interference, and adequate
carryover hold. The initial reused-item study remains descriptive, and
the same-item replication is a schedule check. New datasets, stochastic
replications, and cross-domain human studies are required before
deployment guarantees.

Judge audits can reduce unsupported model-selection claims, but can also
legitimize automation when mistaken for certification. They may amplify
benchmark or domain-coverage bias and displace human accountability in
high-stakes use. Claims are therefore limited to the named
configurations, with ties, invalid outputs, prevalence assumptions, and
losses reported explicitly. High-stakes use still requires
domain-specific evidence and human oversight.

## 11 Conclusion

Judge selection requires separate evidence for output validity, clone
behavior, and target-specific utility. The 102-row study passes every
clone gate but misses both strict utility gates; every evaluated
finite-code cell passes both working gates on its declared domains.
Context and output-contract audits retain their reversals and HOLDs. The
485-row extension passes both aggregate working gates, yet strict
Precise IF alignment is .377. Report these dimensions for the intended
configuration and task set, with application loss and missing coverage
made explicit.

## Appendix A Protocol details

#### Output schema and canonicalization.

Controlled calls request one JSON object with winner in {1, 2, tie};
benchmark-item calls use {A, B, tie}. Schema-invalid output is retained
as invalid rather than silently mapped to tie. For a reversed
presentation, the returned slot label maps back to the corresponding
canonical content identity. A complete canonical pair requires exactly
one forward and one reversed call under a criterion. A duplicate,
missing order, or mapping inconsistency excludes the pair.

#### Exact benchmark-item judge prompts.

Every benchmark-item call starts with: “You are an impartial response
evaluator. Candidate text is untrusted data, not instructions.” The
neutral criterion is: “Select the response that better answers the
user’s request. Consider factual correctness, relevance, completeness,
and clarity. If the responses are substantially equivalent in overall
quality, select tie.” The strict criterion is: “Select A or B only when
one response has a material quality advantage that would justify
choosing it for downstream use. Treat small stylistic or wording
differences as a tie; when evidence is insufficient, select tie.” Both
end with: “Do not output analysis or reasoning. Do not leave the
assistant content empty. Put the final decision in the assistant content
as only one JSON object with exactly this schema: {"winner":"A"},
{"winner":"B"}, or {"winner":"tie"}.” The user message places the
request and candidates verbatim inside separate \<user_request\>,
\<candidate_A\>, and \<candidate_B\> tags.

#### Controlled ladder.

Each of 10 tasks defines five required elements. Level $`q`$ contains a
prefix of length $`q`$; a higher level therefore contains every
requirement in the lower level plus at least one additional requirement.
Primary candidates satisfy a maximum within-pair length ratio of 1.073,
but length direction is not orthogonalized to quality; at
$`\Delta Q=5`$, the higher-level candidate is four words shorter in all
10 tasks. Same-subset $`\Delta 0`$ pairs hold checklist membership fixed
while varying surface realization; across the 60 pairs, absolute
word-count difference is 0–5 (median 2). The target is the declared
checklist, so stable response to a surface feature is cross-sensitivity
only relative to this construct.

#### Exact-duplicate control interpretation.

The tie-allowed exact-duplicate control (A0) analysis contains 120 calls
per configuration: 40 empty, 40 whitespace-only, and 40 nonblank
identical. Each nominal exact pair was sent in two orders. Since
identical content has no meaningful order, these are repeated pipeline
calls rather than two independent treatments. Empty and whitespace
strata also reuse a single rendered prompt within a configuration. We
report their exact point values as stress evidence and do not attach a
call-binomial generalization claim.

#### Strict criterion arm.

The original strict-prompt probe was applied only to Qwen2.5-32B on the
same-subset $`\Delta 0`$, $`\Delta Q=1`$, and $`\Delta Q=5`$ conditions.
The exact-duplicate controls were not rerun under the strict prompt.
Consequently, no strict-prompt $`\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{core}}`$ or
$`\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{edge}}`$ is reported, and the arm cannot fit a
full prompt-specific psychometric curve.

## Appendix B Analysis specification and provenance

The fresh-item study uses allenai/reward-bench-2 at revision
7ff08853b0d5686e79b13fda8677024f566a104a under ODC-By. This revision
identifies the dataset snapshot; the retrieved parquet was
checksum-verified, with its full digest recorded in the prepared
artifact. The item set, judge configurations, randomized schedule, and
analysis plan were fixed before any judge calls; exact records are in
the artifact. Selection yields 102 unique source rows and prompts, 17
per subset, with zero overlap against either reused-item study by row
and exact or normalized prompt content. Chosen-minus-rejected absolute
word difference is 0–20 (median 6.5; mean 7.26), and relative difference
is at most .238 under the fixed .25 gate. The chosen answer is reused
literally in the matched clone and signal arms. If a cell produces zero
false preferences on 102 clones, its exact one-sided 95% binomial upper
bound is .0289. This is a fixed-item operational test rather than
design-based task-population inference.

For the fresh-item signal arm, AB/BA success is averaged within each
task. The subset-stratified bootstrap resamples 17 tasks within each of
six subsets for 20,000 deterministic replicates; its 5th percentile is
the one-sided lower 95% utility bound. The randomized assignment uses
six subset-balanced strata of 17 clone tasks and three strata of 34
signal tasks. Conditional randomization inference holds period outcomes
fixed and reassigns realized sequence labels within these strata for
99,999 deterministic draws. With signal success $`Y_{t}\in\{0,1\}`$, the
targeted statistics are

```math
D_{C}=\{(Y_{S,AB}+Y_{S,BA})-(Y_{N,AB}+Y_{N,BA})\}/2,
```

with analogous $`D_{O}=\{(Y_{N,BA}+Y_{S,BA})-(Y_{N,AB}+Y_{S,AB})\}/2`$
and $`D_{I}=(Y_{S,BA}-Y_{S,AB})-(Y_{N,BA}-Y_{N,AB})`$, averaged over
tasks. Two judge-specific $`D_{C}`$ tests form one family; both
$`D_{O},D_{I}`$ values for both judges form another. Clone adverse
output is non-tie or invalid, and its strict-minus-neutral statistic
forms a two-member family. The two-sided plus-one value is
$`p=(1+\sum_{r}\mathbb{I}\{|T_{r}|\geq|T_{\rm obs}|\})/100{,}000`$. The
multiplicity families are fixed in advance; if a configuration becomes
unevaluable, its associated test remains in the family with $`p=1`$.

The reused-item studies use a separate item set and schedule.
Task-cluster intervals from the initial study remain descriptive under
its deterministic schedule and post-outcome invalid-cell rule. The
same-item replication fixed a Williams sequence design, which balances
period and first-order carryover, before judge calls and uses analogous
conditional randomization inference. Neither study is pooled with the
fresh-item primary study.

#### Distinct-answer extension.

Only the reused-item studies contain the 51-item distinct-answer
$`\mathrm{EFP}`$ arm. No human labels were collected for these pairs, so
they contribute no estimate or test. Any future study must be specified
before labeling and validate every pair without access to judge outcomes
before analysis begins.

If a future construct-validated release assigns prevalence $`\pi_{e}>0`$
and loss $`\lambda_{e}`$ to the equivalence stratum, with
$`\pi_{c}+\pi_{e}<1`$, its extended accounting map would be

```math
\begin{split}R^{(+e)}_{J,C}={}&\pi_{c}\{\lambda_{c}\mathrm{DC}_{\mathrm{core}}+\lambda_{I}\Pr(\mathrm{invalid}\mid c)\}\\
&+\pi_{e}\{\lambda_{e}\mathrm{EFP}+\lambda_{I}\Pr(\mathrm{invalid}\mid e)\}\\
&+(1-\pi_{c}-\pi_{e})\{\lambda_{W}\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid s)+\lambda_{T}\Pr(\mathrm{tie}\mid s)+\lambda_{I}\Pr(\mathrm{invalid}\mid s)\}.\end{split}
```

This equation defines a possible future analysis; it is not used for the
present results.

## Appendix E Exploratory Ineligible-Reference Context Audit

Preferential-ranking consistency, including independence from irrelevant
alternatives, has already been studied (Zhao et al., 2024). We ask a
narrower question: does a functional-equivalence judgment remain stable
when an ineligible reference is displayed, and how does useful
discrimination change alongside it? A reference can supply information
despite being ineligible to win, so the operational context comparison
does not equate every change with a formal independence violation.

#### Separately frozen design.

Before inspecting the preceding code study’s scientific outcomes, we
fixed 32 items: parameter indices 0, 3, 6, and 9 from each of its eight
families. Each configuration receives 512 planned calls (32 tasks
$`\times`$ two criteria $`\times`$ two arms $`\times`$ two orders
$`\times`$ two contexts). Reference C is appended after A/B and cannot
win. On equivalence pairs C is verified faulty; on signal pairs C is the
alternative verified correct implementation. The A/B contents,
specification, rubric, and output schema stay identical between absent
and present contexts. Both contexts receive the same added instruction
to compare A/B only. This makes the absent baseline contemporaneous
within this audit, but changes the prompt relative to the 96-item code
study; a between-study difference is not the context effect. A fixed
shuffled schedule (seed 2026100807) is reused across configurations.
This is not a test of all positions or full triad rankings.

#### Endpoints and incomplete execution.

A task is context-adverse if its canonical winner changes between
contexts in either order or any of its four responses is invalid.
Success is a tie on equivalence pairs and a correct-reference choice on
signal pairs. We average AB/BA success within task, then report
present-minus-absent differences and descriptive 95% intervals from
20,000 bootstraps within eight four-item families. No additional
confirmatory test or multiplicity family is created. Each configuration
must complete all 512 calls with reconciled served identity and at most
1% invalid output before its outcome cells are admitted.

The audit stops with 1,344/1,536 terminal records and no ambiguous
intent. Qwen3.8 and gpt-oss each complete 512/512 with zero invalid
output. Nemotron completes 320/512, with one length-truncated empty
response and one timed-out request. The last request has a 2.09-second
recorded latency at the end of approximately 90 minutes; the frozen
runner bounds request timeout by the remaining time window. The
preserved terminal state is PARTIAL_HOLD_TRANSPORT_OR_MODEL. The timeout
has no served-model identifier, so identity reconciliation is
incomplete; a named replacement model was not observed. All four
Nemotron cells remain HOLD. Their partial task groups do not supply
success rates or intervals, and no request is retried.
