# LLM-as-a-Judge Version Shift: The Instrument Changed, Not the Standard

Guijia Zhang Affiliation: The Hong Kong University Affiliation: of
Science and Technology    Harry Yang Affiliation: The Hong Kong
University Affiliation: of Science and Technology

###### Abstract

Automated research pipelines score proposals with an LLM reviewer and
replace that reviewer when a newer checkpoint ships. A common planning
assumption is that the new judge is harsher. We test that assumption in
a preregistered, weight-frozen design. Three same-family open-weight
transitions, Qwen, Gemma, and Mistral, score a frozen pool of ML
proposals. The assumption does not hold. Qwen $`3.5\!\to\!3.6`$ moves
toward leniency on anchors and on the pool, and that shift survives FDR
correction. Gemma $`3\!\to\!4`$ is strict on constructed anchors and
attenuates on the pool, so its direction is only suggestive.
Mistral-Small $`3.1\!\to\!3.2`$ also has a more lenient mean. That mean
is the wrong summary: $`93\%`$ of pool scores land on a single value,
and $`59\%`$ of the top-$`30\%`$ selection set changes. Giving both
versions the same low-level facts, or the same defect certificates, does
not close the remaining gap. A matched placebo attributes the small fact
effect to packet format rather than content. Global monotone
recalibration repairs the mean and almost none of the object-level
dispersion. The outcomes fall into three failure classes of a
measurement function: a moved origin, a change in object order, and a
loss of resolution. Each class requires a different action. A stratified
anchor audit of about $`200`$ calls classifies the class before the new
judge scores anything that matters. Thresholds frozen from that protocol
issue the matching diagnosis on two later API pairs. We release six
preregistration locks and $`14{,}882`$ judge calls.

## 1 Introduction

Pipelines that use an LLM as a reviewer eventually replace that
reviewer. The prompts, the score threshold, and the rest of the stack
stay put; only the weights change. The change is treated as an upgrade.
In a frozen replication of one such swap,
Mistral-Small $`3.1\!\to\!3.2`$, $`59\%`$ of the top-$`30\%`$ selection
set turns over and the new judge assigns $`93\%`$ of proposals the same
score. The pipeline still returns numbers. It is no longer measuring.

The usual explanation is that newer models are stricter. That story now
informs planning, from leaderboard maintenance to autonomous research
systems that iterate proposals against a judge (Yu et al., 2025; Lu et
al., 2024). Recency does not imply a direction. A mean shift is also the
wrong summary when the instrument has stopped discriminating.

The closest prior work already treats evaluator replacement as a
measurement problem (Yang et al., 2026). That audit compares capability
tiers and served APIs on pairwise judgment datasets and shows that
upgrades are not interchangeable. It does not freeze adjacent
same-family revision hashes, does not randomize the information shown to
both versions, and does not separate a mean shift from a rank reversal
or a collapse of resolution. Those distinctions decide the fix.
Recalibrating a location shift is cheap. Recalibrating a reordering
cannot recover the pairs that traded places. Recalibrating a collapsed
score channel is vacuous.

We treat a judge version as a measurement function. When the weights
change, three things can move independently: the origin of the scale,
the direction in which criteria are combined, and the resolution of the
score map. Each movement has a distinct observable and a different
repair. A reliability audit that reports only a mean cannot tell them
apart. Section 3 states the model. The experiments then do two jobs that
a mean-only audit cannot. They freeze the objects and the weights, so
the score difference is a property of the instrument. They give both
versions the same information, so a residual gap cannot be explained as
a difference in what the two models already know. Figure 1 summarizes
the design and the outcomes.

Design. We freeze three same-family open-weight transitions and a pool
of ML proposals with reviewer-verified defects (Ho et al., 2026). Each
judge scores each object under randomized, stateless conditions on an
information ladder: rubric only, identical frozen facts, and a minimal
defect certificate, with a matched placebo at every rung. Every stage
was locked before any judge output of that stage was generated (Nosek et
al., 2018). Section 4 gives the protocol.

Findings.

1.  1.  Recency predicts neither sign nor quality. Qwen $`3.5\!\to\!3.6`$
        moves toward leniency by about $`0.3`$ SD on both anchors and the
        pool. Mistral $`3.1\!\to\!3.2`$ collapses the score channel:
        $`93\%`$ of pool scores sit on one value. Gemma $`3\!\to\!4`$ is
        strict on constructed anchors and attenuates on the pool, so an
        anchor-only audit can overstate a shift. Three transitions are not a
        population of drift. Section 5.
2.  2.  The residual gap is not a knowledge gap. Identical facts close at
        most $`0.14`$ SD, and a placebo attributes even that to packet
        format. Certificates help one chain and fail a preregistered
        replication. A global map can repair a mean and absorbs under
        $`8\%`$ of object-level dispersion. On Gemma the pool gap flips sign
        across quality tertiles. That pattern is what a global severity
        parameter cannot represent. Section 6.
3.  3.  A small in-domain audit can classify the class. About $`200`$
        stratified anchor calls ask, in order, whether resolution collapsed,
        whether ranks moved beyond same-version noise, or whether only the
        origin moved. The actions are to withhold the new judge, to rescore,
        or to calibrate. The thresholds are a protocol proposal, formalized
        after stage-2 data existed. On two later API pairs the frozen rule
        issues the matching diagnosis. Section 7.

Recognition and revision-loop audits are in the appendix.
Mistral $`3.2`$ collapses on proposals and does not on code review:
reliability is a property of the operating point, not of the model name.

The contributions are a preregistered measurement that falsifies the
harshness rule in both direction and quality; a placebo-controlled
information ladder that bounds the usual contamination and context
objections; a model that separates origin, order, and resolution
failures; and a stratified anchor-audit protocol whose preregistered
nulls are reported as exclusion bounds (Schuirmann, 1987; Lakens, 2017).

## 2 Related work

Judge replacement. Yang et al. (2026) is the closest prior audit. They
document evaluator-replacement ambiguity on four pairwise judgment
datasets, comparing Qwen3 scaling from $`1.7`$B to $`32`$B with adjacent
MiniMax API releases. Upgrades are not interchangeable. Only the
$`1.7`$B to $`4`$B step is a robust accuracy gain, and adjacent API
releases are not. They recommend dataset slices, bias probes, and
protocol logs.

Four design choices separate that audit from this one. They compare
capability tiers and served APIs. We freeze revision hashes on adjacent
same-family pairs, including two same-size patches. They hold the item
fixed and vary the judge. We also randomize the information shown to
both versions, with a placebo at every rung, so a residual gap is not a
gap in context. They do not report a scoring-channel collapse with
intact rationales. An accuracy delta would miss Mistral $`3.2`$. They do
not split remediations by failure class.

Score instability over time and across versions is also documented in
scientific ideation and production judges (Zhang et al., 2025; Singh et
al., 2025; Liu, 2026). Related work adds anchor-based attribution (Li, 2026) and psychometric reliability audits (Norman et al., 2026; Zheng et
al., 2023). Those studies establish that drift exists. They do not say
what the gap is made of, whether supplying the same facts closes it, or
which repair is even possible.

Contamination. Relatedness between a generator and an evaluator can leak
preference (Li et al., 2025). Object-in-training-data contamination of
judges has been a stated worry without a measurement. Our recognition
probe with title verification measures an observable trace and bounds
its contribution. Recognition is not membership evidence, and we do not
treat it as such.

Cue interventions. Placebo-controlled cue studies exist for judge
rationalization (Tapwal et al., 2026). Noise schedules (Khomiakov and
Frellsen, 2026), reference conflicts (Lee et al., 2026), and injected
text (Zhou et al., 2025) all move scores. Our packets are randomized,
non-evaluative, and provenance-carrying, with matched placebos, because
the estimand is a channel effect rather than attack success. Three of
our own effects would have been over-claimed without those arms.

Calibration and anchors. Many-facet models fit severity differences
after the fact (Choi et al., 2026; Casabianca and Beiting-Parrish,
2026). Fixed-parameter calibration extends benchmarks over time (Habba
et al., 2026). Anchor choice itself moves rankings (Don-Yehiya et al.,
2026). Corrector choice is an anchor-budget question (Morandi, 2026).
The simplification those tools share is that a two-parameter map of old
scores onto new ones is enough. That is what we test and reject
off-anchor. Fitting a mean is not recovering a ranking.

Overoptimization. Imperfect proxies can be farmed (Gao et al., 2023;
Zhou, 2026; Kim, 2026; Elganayni et al., 2026). At a stateless,
six-round operating point we do not detect moderate-to-large
judge-specific residue. Appendix E reports the bounds. Shared-context
optimizers remain outside the tested envelope (Pan et al., 2024).

## 3 A measurement model of version shift

Write a judge version $`v`$ as a map from an object $`x`$ to a score

```math
s_{v}(x)=g_{v}\!\left(w_{v}^{\top}z(x)+b_{v}\right), \tag{1}
```

where $`z(x)`$ collects latent criterion-level assessments of $`x`$,
including soundness, evidence, and novelty; $`w_{v}`$ weights those
criteria; $`b_{v}`$ is an offset; and $`g_{v}`$ is strictly increasing.
Replacing the weights can move any of the three parameters. Each
movement has a distinct observable, and each requires a different
action. Table 1 records the correspondence. A reliability audit that
reports only a mean cannot tell the three classes apart.

If only $`b_{v}`$ moves, every object shifts by the same amount on the
latent scale. The mean score changes. Ranks at a fixed threshold may
move near the cut, but the order of objects is preserved. A monotone
recalibration of old scores recovers the new scores. This is an _origin_
change.

If the direction of $`w_{v}`$ changes, objects are reweighted and some
pairs reverse order. No function of the old score alone can restore
those pairs: two objects with the same old score can carry different new
scores. This is an _order_ change. Appendix M records a sufficient
condition under which a global monotone map
$`s_{\mathrm{new}}=h(s_{\mathrm{old}})`$ exists. We do not treat that
condition as identified. $`z(x)`$ is unobserved, so the data classify
the kind of change, not which criterion weights moved.

If the slope of $`g_{v}`$ flattens over the realized range,
discrimination collapses. Upstream channels can remain responsive. The
model can still list defects and write object-specific rationales while
emitting a constant scalar. Recalibrating a constant is vacuous. This is
a _resolution_ failure. The instrument has to be withheld, or the
operating point changed.

These three readings are an idealization. What the design identifies is
the class of change, through the signatures in Table 1. A second
question is causal rather than descriptive. A gap that is only a
difference in what the two versions already know should close when both
are given the same facts. A gap that is a change in the response
function should not. The information ladder tests that alternative. It
starts from a bare rubric, then adds identical frozen facts, then a
defect certificate, each with a matched placebo. If the residual after
clamping is still object-dependent, the swap changed the instrument, not
the evidence.

Section 5 reports which class each transition instantiates. Section 6
tests the knowledge alternative. Section 7 asks whether a cheap
in-domain audit can detect the class before the new judge scores
anything that matters.

| Class      | What moves | Signature                              | Required action |
| ---------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Origin     | $`b_{v}`$  | location shift, order intact           | calibrate       |
| Order      | $`w_{v}`$  | rank reversals, residual dispersion    | rescore         |
| Resolution | $`g_{v}`$  | score concentration, intact rationales | withhold        |

Table 1: Three failure classes of a version change. The design
classifies the class through these signatures. It does not identify the
latent weights.

## 4 Experimental design

The design has two jobs: classify each transition against Table 1, and
test whether the gap is a difference in what the two versions already
know.

Judges. Three same-family open-weight transitions are frozen by revision
hash: Qwen 3.5-27B$`\to`$3.6-27B, Gemma 3-27B$`\to`$4-31B, and
Mistral-Small 3.1-24B$`\to`$3.2-24B. The Qwen and Mistral pairs are
same-size patch revisions. Gemma is a generation jump with a size
change. We treat all three as the kind of replacement a pipeline
actually performs, not as exchangeable effect sizes.

Decoding is fixed at temperature $`0.6`$ and top-$`p`$ $`0.95`$, with
per-call CRC seeds. That setting matches a common deployment choice
rather than greedy decoding. Prompt-repeat reliability is
quadratic-weighted $`\kappa=0.86`$–$`0.93`$. A later check shows the
Mistral collapse is stable across temperature. A pool-scale second pass
separates sampling noise from version change. Section 7 and Appendix J
report those checks.

Objects. The pool has 348 frozen proposal-style objects derived from ML
conference submissions from 2022 to 2026, with reviewer soundness scores
and verified risk factors (Ho et al., 2026). They fall in three disjoint
sets. The first is 48 anchors, of which 12 are clean, 12 are flawed, and
24 are natural. The second is an 80-object intervention pool. The third
is a 220-object expansion pool spanning five submission years. Full pool
means the 300 non-anchor objects. The 62 masked-title variants are
rewrites of these objects, not additional samples. All bootstraps
cluster at the object level. Appendix H gives the call counts.

The ladder. For each object we issue stateless randomized calls.
Condition B is commit-first defect listing with no score, following Zhou
(2026). Condition R is a title-verified recognition probe. Condition L0
is the rubric only. Condition L1 adds a frozen atomic-fact packet, built
by a neutral third family and identical for both judges. Condition L1P
is a matched placebo packet. Condition L2 adds a minimal defect
certificate distilled from reviewer risk factors and leakage-filtered.
Condition L2P is a donor-mismatched certificate matched for length,
style, and authority cues. Qwen and Gemma run the full ladder. Mistral
was added at stage 2 as a third-family replication and runs L0, R, B,
L2, and L2P, with no fact conditions.

For condition $`C`$, let
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(C)=\overline{(s_{\mathrm{old}}-s_{\mathrm{new}})}\,/\,\sigma_{L0}`$
denote the standardized version gap under $`C`$, an object-clustered
mean, where $`\sigma_{L0}`$ is the transition’s pooled L0 SD. Positive
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}`$ means the new judge is stricter. The
preregistered estimands are
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}\equiv\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathrm{L0})`$,
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{fact}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathrm{L0})-\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathrm{L1})`$,
and
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}=\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathrm{L1})-\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathrm{L2})`$.

Preregistration. Six locks, one per stage, freeze configs, prompts, code
hashes, kill gates, and analysis scripts before any judge output of that
stage. Seventeen amendments are logged in Appendix I. Each was
documented before unblinding the affected endpoint. Amendments extend
scope or add replications; they do not redefine a confirmatory endpoint
after unblinding. Every test is registered as confirmatory or
exploratory in Table 4. Negative results carry TOST exclusion bounds and
80%-power minimum detectable effects. Appendix B reports those bounds.

Qwen is predominantly an origin change. It moves toward leniency by
$`0.31`$ SD on anchors and $`0.33`$ SD on the pool. Both intervals
exclude zero.

Mistral is a resolution failure. On the pool, $`93\%`$ of scores sit on
one value, clean–flawed separation falls from $`0.73`$ to $`0.24`$, and
$`86\%`$ of pairs are tied. The SD-standardized mean is $`-0.65`$ on
anchors and $`-0.45`$ on the pool. That number is a fragile summary of a
collapsed channel, so we lead with concentration and rank statistics
rather than with leniency. The collapse is a finding about instrument
risk. That chain is excluded from the calibration claims of Section 7.
Only its degradation flag enters the protocol table.

Gemma is strict on the constructed anchors, $`+0.30`$ SD, and much
smaller on the pool, $`+0.11`$. The pool interval includes zero. We
treat the direction as suggestive.

The harshness claim is universal, so the two FDR-surviving shifts toward
leniency already refute it. We do not claim that leniency is typical.

Anchor estimates are audit statistics, not pool estimates. Anchors are a
constructed set, so every headline gap is also reported on the expansion
pool, and for Mistral on the 300-object full pool, sampled without
regard to defect status. Table 2 and Appendix F.6 give the numbers.
Qwen’s magnitude agrees. Gemma’s attenuation is the reason we call its
direction suggestive, and a warning that an anchor-only audit can
overstate a shift.

Multiplicity. As a sensitivity analysis we apply Benjamini–Hochberg FDR
correction across the eleven scalar confirmatory endpoints. Appendix F
lists them. The Qwen location shift survives, $`p<10^{-3}`$. The Mistral
mean shift also survives, but we do not read it as a leniency finding.
Rank statistics on the anchors show $`46\%`$ exact ties, rank-biserial
$`0.74`$, and Wilcoxon $`p=4\times 10^{-4}`$. Gemma’s direction toward
strictness, $`p\approx 0.045`$, does not survive FDR, matching the pool
attenuation. No other conclusion changes.

The collapse is in the scalar score, not in the harness or the reading.
Three audits localize Mistral 3.2. Appendix F reports them in full. The
harness is intact: $`0`$ of $`252`$ pool calls fail to parse, output
lengths are ordinary, and there is no repetition pathology.
Comprehension is intact: justifications are object-specific, with mean
pairwise 3-gram Jaccard $`0.047`$. The model reads each proposal, writes
a tailored rationale, and then emits the same score. Discrimination is
also intact on the commit-first B probe. Mistral 3.2’s defect counts
separate clean from flawed anchors by $`0.55`$ SD, Cliff’s
$`\delta=0.30`$, numerically better than its predecessor at
$`-0.27`$ SD. The mapping to a scalar is what broke.

A preregistered perturbation matrix, Table 10, completes the
attribution. The collapse is stable across decoding temperature. It
partly recovers under a semantically equivalent prompt paraphrase, with
SD rising from $`0.31`$ to $`0.53`$, still at least $`42\%`$ below the
predecessor. The same paraphrase leaves the predecessor unchanged. The
saturation binds to prompt surface form. Only an in-domain audit will
show which operating point still works.

The collapse is also domain-specific. On $`48`$ code-review objects the
same pair moves toward leniency by $`-0.79`$ SD with discrimination
intact. Appendix L reports that study.

The sign of a working instrument’s mean shift tracks where the _old_
judge was miscalibrated. Old Qwen under-scored clean objects. Old Gemma
over-scored flawed ones. Reading that sign requires anchored ground
truth, which is why Section 7 scores a frozen in-domain set before the
new judge is trusted.

## 6 What does not close the gap

Figure 3: The information ladder. Version gap, old minus new, in
pooled-SD units, with means and 95% CIs, under L0, L1, and L2. L0 is the
rubric. L1 adds identical frozen facts. L2 adds a defect certificate.
Open diamonds mark the matched placebos at each rung. Facts barely move
either chain’s gap, and the placebos track the real interventions. The
placebo-controlled contrast attributes what little the packets do to
format, not content, as Table 14 reports. Certificates shrink the gap in
Qwen but fail a preregistered Mistral replication, as Table 6 reports.

Section 3 left a causal alternative: the gap could be a difference in
what the two versions already know. The information ladder tests that
alternative. If identical facts or identical certificates restored the
old scores, the swap would be an evidence gap rather than an instrument
change.

Non-evaluative fact supply does not close the gap. Clamping identical
frozen fact packets moves the pooled gap by
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{fact}}=+0.060`$ SD. The interval is
$`[-0.039,+0.158]`$, on $`128`$ paired objects on the two fact-bearing
chains, sign-aligned. Equivalence testing excludes effects of
$`0.143`$ SD or larger. Identical low-level context is not a fix.

The active ingredient is format, not content. Irrelevant placebo facts
already perturb Qwen anchors as strongly as real ones, $`0.33`$ versus
$`0.04`$ SD, so L1 confounds content with packet form. A preregistered
placebo-controlled extension separates the channels. L1P is placed on
every L1-covered object. The sample is $`128`$ paired objects. Table 14
reports the contrast. The content-specific effect is null, pooled
$`-0.088`$ SD, interval $`[-0.190,+0.012]`$. TOST excludes effects of
$`0.174`$ SD or larger. A placebo packet alone moves the gap toward zero
by $`0.11`$–$`0.17`$ SD, significantly on Qwen and numerically more than
the real facts do.

The packets are model-extracted, leakage-filtered atomic facts. They
lexically cover about $`5\%`$ of reviewer-verified risk factors, against
$`29\%`$ for certificates. Appendix G details the construction. What the
data rule out is that low-level facts or auto-generated certificates
generally close the gap. Expert-curated evidence remains untested.

Certificates do not generally bypass. Table 6 carries the numbers. The
preregistered primary is pooled $`\Delta_{\mathrm{diag}}`$ on flawed
objects. It is inconclusive under our own bar, and the placebo gate did
not trigger. An exploratory paired contrast that cancels object noise
came out significant. A preregistered third-family replication on
Mistral returned null. That replication used the same 33 flawed objects,
with sign, scale, and a one-sided test frozen in advance, and the
manipulation check passed. The certificate channel is chain-specific at
best. Mismatched pseudo-certificates still depress clean-object scores
in every judge, three times more strongly in old Qwen, $`-0.95`$ versus
$`-0.30`$ raw. Version replacement changes the response to
authoritative-sounding text, not just severity.

Prior exposure is the weakest finding. Title-verified recognition
differs across versions. New Qwen recognizes $`29\%`$ of objects,
against $`12\%`$ for old Qwen. Gemma 3 claims recognition it cannot
verify $`36\%`$ of the time, so self-report is not an audit. Verified
recognition establishes prior exposure or reconstruction, not
membership. Recognized objects carry an excess gap of $`0.155`$ SD under
L0, shrinking to $`0.04`$ SD under fact-clamping. The strata are
observational and imbalanced. Recognized objects are older and
higher-scored, with SMD $`0.49`$–$`0.73`$. Appendix F.3 reports the
balance table.

Three checks are consistent with an information advantage and no more.
The excess persists under placebo packets. Gap dispersion is comparable
across strata. A masked-title difference-in-differences is directionally
consistent but underpowered, $`+0.095`$, and the interval contains zero.

Global maps repair the mean, not the objects. After clamping, about
$`77\%`$ of both chains’ gaps survive. We treat what follows as residual
attribution, not mechanism identification. The preregistered
two-parameter model is
$`s_{\mathrm{new}}\approx\gamma\,s_{\mathrm{old}}+\delta`$, fitted on 24
anchors and required to explain at least two thirds of the off-anchor
mean gap. It failed on both chains. Qwen overshoots, explained fraction
$`-0.15`$. Gemma extrapolates, $`\gamma=2.5`$, explained fraction
$`-12.5`$. Part of the Gemma failure is an anchor artifact. The old
judge compresses anchors into a two-point range, so the slope is
unidentified (Don-Yehiya et al., 2026).

Refitting on random subsets of the 220-object pool, 200 draws at
$`k=24`$, $`40`$, and $`80`$, shows the failure is not only about
anchors. On Qwen the mean becomes correctable, with median explained
fraction $`0.76`$–$`0.81`$ for $`k\geq 40`$. On Gemma the two-thirds
gate still fails in $`77\%`$ of draws at $`k=80`$. On both chains a
linear map fitted on the full pool absorbs less than $`8\%`$ of
object-level gap dispersion. Appendix F.2 reports the refit.

The Gemma pool gap itself flips sign across reviewer overall-score
tertiles. The gap is $`+0.80`$ SD on weak objects, $`-0.20`$ in the
middle, and $`-0.43`$ on strong ones. Qwen is uniformly lenient, at
$`-0.23`$, $`-0.37`$, and $`-0.41`$. A global severity parameter cannot
represent a sign flip of that kind. Appendix F.6 reports the subgroup
table.

These are the signatures of Section 3. Qwen is an origin-dominant shift
whose mean is repairable off-anchor and whose objects are not. Gemma is
an order change. The tertile reversal is the signature that a global map
cannot represent. Mistral remains a resolution failure. The scalar
channel collapsed while rationales and defect lists did not. Rank
reversals exceed the same-version noise floor on every chain, as
Section 7 reports. Where the Gemma gap is reliable, split-half
$`r=0.81`$, and observable features predict $`46\%`$ of its variance.
Compressing length or stripping rhetoric leaves the gap unchanged.
Appendix K has those audits. The change binds to content properties that
survive both edits. Estimating $`\Delta w`$ would require per-criterion
interventions with matched placebo edits. Section 8 returns to that
experiment.

## 7 A pre-deployment compatibility test

The three classes in Section 3 need three different actions. Table 3 is
the protocol we propose. The questions are asked in that order. A single
drift statistic conflates failures that require opposite remediations.

| Class      | Ask                      | Trigger                                | Action                |
| ---------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------- |
| Resolution | Score channel collapsed? | modal share $`>0.5`$ or sep. $`<0.30`$ | Do not deploy         |
| Order      | Ranks moved?             | $`\tau`$ below both test–retest floors | Rescore or freeze old |
| Origin     | Origin only?             | boundary disagreement $`>10\%`$        | Calibrate on anchors  |

Table 3: Pre-deployment compatibility test on stratified in-domain
anchors, aligned with Table 1. Thresholds were set by convention and
formalized after stage-2 data existed. They are a protocol proposal.
Appendix F.7 reports a leave-one-transition-out check. That check shows
insensitivity, not external validity. The two API pairs test the
diagnosis, not the realized payoff of every prescribed action.

Separation alone is not a reliable flag at the boundary. Anchor
resampling leaves Qwen and Gemma untouched but puts collapsed Mistral
near the $`0.30`$ threshold, with bootstrap flag rate $`51\%`$. Score
concentration sits far from any boundary: $`93\%`$ of scores on a single
value and $`86\%`$ tied pairs. That is why Table 3 pairs the two flags.

Scalar maps and rank pipelines. Fitted on 40 anchors and evaluated on
the non-anchor pool, ordinal probit improves Qwen MAE from $`0.833`$ to
$`0.811`$ and CRPS from $`0.65`$ to $`0.57`$. On Gemma every mapping
underperforms identity, as the tertile reversal predicts. Monotone maps
leave within-chain Kendall $`\tau`$ unchanged, so a rank-based pipeline
can skip scalar recalibration of a _fixed_ judge. The exemption does not
cover the transition. Replacing the judge reorders objects. Table 2
reports $`\tau=0.61`$, $`0.51`$, and $`0.18`$, and $`37`$–$`59\%`$ of
the top-$`30\%`$ set flips.

A second L0 pass over every pool object measures the sampling floor.
Same-version test–retest $`\tau`$ is $`0.68`$–$`0.82`$, and $`0.34`$ for
pre-collapse Mistral. Cross-version $`\tau`$ falls below both endpoints
on all three chains. Version change adds $`+12`$, $`+35`$, and $`+14`$
flip points beyond the same-version mean for Qwen, Gemma, and Mistral.
Appendix J reports the floor. Rank pipelines still have to audit rank
stability, boundary crossings, and discrimination at each swap.

Equal-budget migration. Figure 4 selects the new-judge top-$`30\%`$
under a fixed rescoring budget. On Qwen, isotonic anchor recalibration
lifts F1 from $`0.777`$ to $`0.833`$ and cuts regret by $`40\%`$. Regret
is the shortfall in mean reviewer soundness against the best same-size
selection. Beyond the anchor budget a Bayesian posterior layer reaches
F1 $`0.901`$–$`0.944`$ at budgets of $`80`$ to $`150`$. Isotonic keeps
the better regret, $`0.32`$ versus $`0.46`$. Fidelity to the incoming
judge, measured by F1, and fidelity to external quality, measured by
regret, are separate objectives. Best arm refers to F1. On Gemma the
naive policy already has regret near zero, because the shift misses the
selection boundary. The diagnose-then-act rule in Table 3 matches the
post-hoc best F1 arm on the two non-degenerate chains and raises the
degradation flag on Mistral. We count only Qwen and Gemma as calibration
evidence. Mistral’s F1 comparison is trivial.

Independent API pairs. With thresholds frozen, pinned snapshots GPT-5.4
$`\to`$ GPT-5.5 scored the 48 anchors under the identical L0 prompt. The
shift is $`0.45`$ SD toward strictness, interval $`[0.32,0.57]`$.
Separation stays intact, $`3.52`$ to $`3.11`$. Boundary disagreement is
$`12.5\%`$. The rule triggers calibration and withholds the degradation
flag. A pool-scale rescore of 660 calls, with endpoints frozen first,
confirms the diagnosis out of sample. Pool
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=0.73`$ SD, interval $`[0.64,0.82]`$, and
disagreement is $`33\%`$. The payoff is pipeline-dependent.
Anchor-fitted isotonic calibration cuts boundary disagreement to
$`19\%`$. A monotone map cannot alter top-$`k`$ selection, so the frozen
fidelity endpoint is null by construction. Appendix J reports that
check.

A second blind pair from a second provider, DeepSeek flash $`\to`$ pro,
is a tier replacement rather than a same-size patch. It returns a
$`-0.42`$ SD shift toward leniency, interval $`[-0.60,-0.25]`$, with
improved separation from $`1.78`$ to $`2.12`$. The rule again withholds
the degradation flag and triggers calibration. DeepSeek was not rescored
at pool scale, so that pair tests diagnosis only.

Scope. The thresholds are an in-simulation demonstration. The two API
pairs are the first independent tests of the diagnosis. Pinned snapshots
are not weight-frozen, and decoding is provider-default. Those estimates
are descriptive. Stress tests of the audit itself, reported in
Appendix K, put the minimum viable size near 24 stratified anchors for
healthy instruments. Near-threshold trigger verdicts should be escalated
to a pool-scale check.

## 8 Limitations and outlook

The primary dataset is proposal-style text. A 48-object code-review
replication shows that sign and magnitude differ by domain. Appendix L
reports that study. Three preregistered transitions plus two descriptive
API pairs refute universal claims and demonstrate the audit. They are
not a population estimate of drift. Cross-family replacement on the same
pool is at least as disruptive and was not a confirmatory endpoint.
Appendix K reports that check.

Criterion assessments are latent, so the geometry reading rests on
observed signatures rather than identified weights. Anchor selection
remains a live failure mode (Don-Yehiya et al., 2026). Pinned API
snapshots are not weight-frozen (Liu, 2026). Fact packets and
certificates are model-generated and non-evaluative by design.
Appendix G states the construction. The revision null is one operating
point. Appendix E states the bound. Recognition is prior exposure, not
membership. The protocol issues the matching diagnosis on both API pairs
and remains unvalidated under cross-family replacement in the field.

Three experiments would close the remaining gaps. Identifying
$`\Delta w`$ needs per-criterion interventions with matched placebo
edits. A field test of Table 3 needs a swap that the authors do not
choose, including a cross-family replacement. Expert-curated evidence
would test whether the fact-channel null is an artifact of
model-extracted packets. Those studies are the next paper.

## 9 Conclusion

Replacing an LLM judge changes the instrument, not a scalar severity.
Origin, criterion direction, and resolution can move independently, and
recency does not pick the sign. Identical facts do not restore the old
scores, and a global map cannot recover the objects that traded places.
A compatibility test on frozen in-domain anchors classifies the class
before the new judge scores anything that matters: withhold when
resolution is gone, rescore when ranks have moved, and calibrate only
when the origin moved. The instrument changed. The standard does not
have to.

## Appendix B Equivalence tests

For each preregistered null, we report the TOST margin (the smallest
$`\Delta`$ such that the 90% bootstrap CI lies within
$`[-\Delta,+\Delta]`$) and the minimum detectable effect at 80% power
($`\mathrm{MDE}_{80}=2.8\times\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{boot}}`$)
(Schuirmann, 1987; Lakens, 2017). An interpretive caveat we adopt
throughout: because these margins are derived from the data rather than
pre-specified as smallest effect sizes of interest (SESOI), they are
_exclusion bounds_, statements that effects at least this large are
ruled out at $`\alpha{=}0.05`$, not equivalence declarations in the
strict Lakens sense. Main-text phrasing (“TOST excludes $`\geq x`$”)
follows this reading; where a bound exceeds what would be a defensible
SESOI (e.g. the Mistral replication, $`\pm 0.427`$), the endpoint should
be read as inconclusive rather than null. The bounds are also
per-endpoint at the tested operating point (e.g. revision depth 6): they
do not constrain the slow accumulation of sub-bound residues across
repeated pipeline generations.

## Appendix E Revision loop

If judges have idiosyncratic thresholds, iterative revision against a
fixed judge can farm those idiosyncrasies and collapse on the next
version (Gao et al., 2023; Elganayni et al., 2026). We preregistered a
fair version of that attack (Figure 5): 40 objects stratified by
measured threshold sensitivity (empirical-Bayes–shrunk residual gap
after the global map), six rounds of revise-with-judge-feedback (neutral
third-family reviser, text-only), a 20-object generic-revision control
arm, fresh-seed rescoring to control winner’s curse, and held-out
evaluation by the other family.

Figure 5: Revision loop at a stateless, six-round operating point.
Effects with 95% bootstrap CIs; shaded bands mark the exclusion boundary
for preregistered nulls. The positive control (fresh-seed gain) passes.
Every exploitation signature is null within the stated bounds.

Gains are real (fresh-seed depth-6 gain $`+0.304`$ SD
$`[+0.096,+0.527]`$) but not judge-specific (judge-feedback beats
generic revision on held-out judges by $`+0.062`$ SD
$`[-0.174,+0.302]`$; TOST excludes $`\geq 0.262`$), not threshold-linked
($`\rho`$(sensitivity, evaporation) $`=0.156`$, $`p{=}0.34`$,
$`n{=}40`$; TOST excludes $`|\rho|\geq 0.41`$), and not seed-farmed
(winner’s curse $`-0.075`$ points $`[-0.300,+0.150]`$). Evaporation is
the non-transferable share of gain.

The judges are stateless, which removes the shared conversational
context that drives in-context reward hacking (Pan et al., 2024). The
claim is scoped: under a stateless, six-round, mild-reviser operating
point, we detect no moderate-to-large judge-specific overfitting. The
bound excludes judge-specific gains $`\geq 0.26`$ SD (80%-power MDE
$`0.34`$). Effects of $`0.10`$–$`0.25`$ SD remain compatible. $`n{=}40`$
powers the correlation test only for $`|\rho|\geq 0.43`$. Optimizers
that share context, run deeper, or accumulate sub-bound residues across
generations are outside the tested envelope. An interim $`n{=}9`$
control contrast looked significant and evaporated at the preregistered
full sample.

### F.7 Controller threshold provenance and leave-one-transition-out check

Provenance. The meta-policy thresholds (anchor separation $`<0.30`$ for
degradation; anchor boundary disagreement $`>10\%`$ for calibration)
were fixed by convention but formalized after stage-2 data existed; the
3/3 match in Figure 4 is therefore an in-simulation demonstration.

Leave-one-transition-out. For each held-out chain we sweep the
disagreement threshold over $`[0.02,0.28]`$ (step $`0.02`$), keep the
values for which the meta-policy matches the post-hoc best arm (within
$`0.01`$ F1) on _both_ training chains, and evaluate the median such
threshold on the held-out chain. Training regions: $`[0.02,0.12]`$ (Qwen
held out), $`[0.02,0.12]`$ (Gemma held out), $`[0.02,0.20]`$ (Mistral
held out); selected thresholds $`0.07/0.07/0.11`$; held-out match: 3 of
3 (on the Mistral fold the F1 match is trivial because scores are
near-constant, but its degradation flag is correctly raised, consistent
with the main-text accounting that counts only the non-degenerate chains
as calibration evidence). The region for which all three chains match
simultaneously is $`[0.02,0.12]`$, and the preregistration-era default
of $`0.10`$ lies inside it. The separation threshold enters only the
degradation flag (Mistral’s $`0.243`$ vs. others’ $`\geq 2.75`$ leaves a
wide margin) and does not affect F1. With three transitions this
establishes threshold _insensitivity_, not external validity;
independent transitions are the real holdout, and amendments F1 and G4D
(Appendix J) supply two, on both of which the frozen thresholds
prescribe the correct action.

Anchor-composition sensitivity. A stratified bootstrap over the anchor
set (clean and flawed anchors resampled separately, $`5{,}000`$ draws)
and leave-one-anchor-out both leave the non-degenerate verdicts
unchanged: Qwen and Gemma separations stay far above the health
threshold (bootstrap flag rate $`0\%`$; LOO ranges $`[2.66,3.44]`$ and
$`[2.90,5.27]`$), and the calibration trigger fires in $`93\%`$ and
$`100\%`$ of draws respectively. The collapsed Mistral chain, by
contrast, sits _near_ the threshold: separation $`0.243`$, bootstrap
flag rate $`51\%`$, LOO range $`[0.03,0.61]`$. The mean-separation flag
is therefore not a reliable sole detector at the boundary; the
score-concentration statistic ($`93\%`$ of pool calls on a single value;
$`86\%`$ tied pairs) sits far from any boundary and is the robust
signal; a deployed audit should raise the degradation flag on either. A
related concern, whether a pathological anchor set could silently pass
the health check, is bounded by construction: the health statistic uses
the _new_ judge’s clean–flawed separation, and the Gemma anchor
pathology (old-judge score compression, which broke the anchor-fitted
_map_) does not touch it; when the map breaks, the disagreement trigger
fires instead (trigger rate $`100\%`$ on Gemma). Analysis:
reviewer3_analyses.py.

## Appendix J API judge pairs and same-version baselines (amendments F1–F3, G4D)

This appendix takes the audit protocol out of sample, in four steps: a
blind anchor audit of a pinned GPT snapshot pair (F1), a pool-scale
rescore of the same pair that tests the prescribed calibration where it
matters (F2), same-version repeated-call baselines that separate version
change from sampling noise for every instrument in the paper (F3), and a
second blind pair from a second provider (G4D). Every design was frozen
before its first call.

Design (frozen before any call). Judges: OpenAI dated snapshots
gpt-5.4-2026-03-05 (old) and gpt-5.5-2026-04-23 (new), the most recent
adjacent dated pair at run time, mirroring the “upgrade to the newest
version” decision the paper studies. Objects: the 48 stage-0a frozen
anchors (12 clean / 12 flawed / 24 natural), content-hash verified.
Condition: L0 only, the stage-0a rubric prompt verbatim. Three repeats
per object per judge (288 calls); the same JSON-final-line parsing rule
as all other chains. The endpoints, the diagnose-then-act thresholds to
be applied (separation $`<0.30`$; boundary disagreement $`>0.10`$), the
pass criterion, and a sampler-parameter fallback rule were frozen in
amendment_F1.json before the first API call. The endpoint rejected
temperature and top_p for these snapshots; per the frozen rule both were
dropped identically for both judges (provider-default decoding).

Results. Zero parse failures in 288 calls. Anchor
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=+0.452`$ SD, object-clustered bootstrap 95% CI
$`[+0.319,+0.572]`$, with pooled L0 SD $`1.57`$ and means
$`5.96\to 5.25`$. The newer snapshot is _stricter_, opposite in sign to
the two robust open-weight shifts and adding an out-of-family instance
of sign unpredictability. Clean–flawed separation moves from $`3.52`$ to
$`3.11`$, far above the $`0.30`$ degradation threshold, with no
Mistral-style collapse. Rank stability is Kendall $`\tau=0.80`$, with
$`17.6\%`$ of anchor pairs exactly tied under the new judge. Boundary
($`\geq 6.0`$) decision disagreement is $`12.5\%`$.

Diagnose-then-act evaluation. At the frozen thresholds the heuristic
withholds the degradation flag, since separation $`3.11\geq 0.30`$, and
triggers calibration, since disagreement $`12.5\%>10\%`$. Post-hoc
inspection of the same data agrees on both counts: the instrument is
healthy, and a $`0.45`$ SD location shift at an intact ranking is
exactly the regime in which anchor calibration pays (§7). This is the
first evaluation of the thresholds on a transition that contributed
nothing to their selection, and the first evidence that the audit
protocol transfers to API-served judges.

Scope. Pinned snapshots are not weight-frozen. The provider can silently
alter serving, so this transition cannot join the confirmatory family
and its estimates are descriptive. Decoding differs from the open-weight
chains, using provider defaults rather than seeded sampling, and
reasoning-mode inference may differ in kind. What the amendment
establishes is protocol transfer and threshold generalization to a
fourth, out-of-family, API-served pair, not a fourth confirmatory
replication. Whether the prescribed calibration realizes a benefit on
this pair requires pool-scale rescoring; amendment F2 supplies it. All
raw responses, the frozen amendment files, and the analysis scripts are
released with the artifacts.

Pool-scale rescore (amendment F2; design frozen before any call). Both
snapshots scored all 220 expansion-pool objects under the verbatim L0
prompt, the new snapshot twice (660 calls; the F1 identical-drop sampler
rule applied from the start; zero parse failures). Pool
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=+0.735`$ SD, object bootstrap 95% CI
$`[+0.641,+0.824]`$ ($`n{=}220`$), larger than the anchor estimate of
$`0.45`$: the anchor audit understated this shift rather than
overstating it. Accept-boundary ($`\geq 6.0`$) disagreement is
$`32.7\%`$ at pool scale versus $`12.5\%`$ on anchors, so the
calibration trigger fires more strongly out of sample and confirms the
F1 diagnosis. The payoff is pipeline-dependent. The frozen
selection-fidelity endpoint, top-30% F1 of calibrated-old versus
naive-old selection against the new-judge policy, returns exactly equal
values ($`0.727`$). An isotonic map with the frozen tie-break is
monotone and cannot alter a top-$`k`$ selection, so the frozen
vindication rule returns null and we report it as such. The measurable
payoff is at the score threshold: anchor-fitted isotonic calibration
cuts accept-boundary disagreement from $`32.7\%`$ to $`19.1\%`$, a
post-hoc descriptive contrast computed after the frozen endpoints were
evaluated. Same-version baseline on the API pair, from two independent
new-snapshot passes: test–retest $`\tau=0.824`$, top-30% flip
$`18.2\%`$, against cross-version $`\tau=0.661`$, flip $`27.3\%`$. The
version-attributable excess is about nine points, replicating the
open-weight pattern below on a frontier API chain.

Same-version repeated-call baselines (post hoc). The main-text
rank-instability statistics (Table 2) are computed from one call per
judge per object, so they conflate version change with sampling noise.
Using the three frozen anchor L0 repeats per judge, mean pairwise
single-call test–retest is: Qwen $`\tau_{\mathrm{rep}}=0.78`$ (old) /
$`0.72`$ (new); Gemma $`0.62`$ / $`0.83`$; Mistral $`0.37`$ / $`0.55`$;
API pair $`0.89`$ / $`0.92`$. Like-for-like cross-version comparisons,
single call versus single call on the same anchors, give $`\tau=0.65`$
(Qwen), $`0.47`$ (Gemma), $`0.23`$ (Mistral), and $`0.85`$ (API), below
the corresponding same-version baselines on every chain, with the
smallest margin on Qwen. Amendment F3 upgrades this anchor-level bound
to a direct pool-level decomposition. The residual lesson stands:
single-call scoring is a noisy instrument, and an audit should use
repeated calls, as our anchor protocol does.

Pool-level same-version baselines for the open-weight chains (amendment
F3; design frozen before any call). One independent second L0 pass over
every pool object per judge (1,384 calls; prompts, decoding, and the
per-call seed rule identical to the frozen stage-2 pool protocol;
$`\leq 2`$ parse failures per judge). Per-judge pool test–retest
$`\tau`$ (top-30% flip): Qwen $`0.70`$ ($`24\%`$) old / $`0.68`$
($`29\%`$) new; Gemma $`0.70`$ ($`24\%`$) / $`0.82`$ ($`11\%`$); Mistral
$`0.34`$ ($`38\%`$) / $`0.76`$ ($`2.7\%`$). Cross-version $`\tau`$ falls
below _both_ endpoints’ own baselines on all three chains ($`0.61`$ vs.
$`\geq 0.68`$; $`0.51`$ vs. $`\geq 0.70`$; $`0.19`$ vs. $`\geq 0.34`$),
and the excess top-30% flip beyond each pair’s same-version mean is
$`+11.9`$ (Qwen), $`+34.8`$ (Gemma), $`+13.8`$ (Mistral) points. Two
artifacts read correctly. Pre-collapse M_old is itself a noisy
instrument, with test–retest $`\tau=0.34`$, so a substantial share of
the Mistral cross-version flip is sampling noise rather than version
change. Collapsed M_new’s $`2.7\%`$ flip reflects the deterministic
tie-break under $`86\%`$ ties, not discrimination. This replaces the
earlier anchor-level upper-bound reading: the main-text pool flip
percentages now decompose into a measured same-version noise floor plus
a version-attributable excess that is positive on every chain.

Cross-fitted calibration check (post hoc, underpowered). A top-$`k`$
payoff check on the anchors alone would be vacuous, because isotonic
maps are monotone and cannot change a rank-based selection of the same
score set; the simulated F1 gains in §7 arise from mixing calibrated old
scores with rescored objects. The deployment decision that monotone
recalibration _does_ move is the fixed accept boundary ($`\geq 6.0`$),
the same boundary the calibration trigger monitors. Cross-fitting on the
F1 anchors (200 random 24/24 splits; isotonic fit on one half, evaluated
on the other): held-out accept-decision agreement with the new judge
rises from $`0.879`$ (uncalibrated) to $`0.909`$ (calibrated), improving
or tying in $`89\%`$ of splits ($`\Delta`$ agreement $`10`$th–$`90`$th
percentile $`[-0.04,+0.13]`$). Directionally positive, but with
24-object evaluation halves this is a consistency check, not a payoff
measurement; the F2 pool-scale rescore above confirms the direction at
$`n{=}220`$ ($`32.7\%\to 19.1\%`$).

A fifth pair from a second provider (amendment G4D; design frozen before
any call). DeepSeek v4-flash $`\to`$ v4-pro is a same-provider _tier
replacement_, the “swap to the better model” decision rather than a
dated-snapshot upgrade. The protocol matches F1: the 48 frozen anchors,
L0 verbatim, three repeats per judge, 288 calls, with zero parse
failures after a logged token-budget escalation retry for the
reasoning-mode flash tier. Anchor replacement gap $`-0.42`$ SD,
object-clustered bootstrap 95% CI $`[-0.60,-0.25]`$, means
$`4.75\to 5.48`$. The newer, more capable tier is _more lenient_, the
opposite sign to the GPT pair on the same objects and prompt, and a
fifth instance of sign unpredictability. Clean–flawed separation
improves ($`1.78\to 2.12`$); Kendall $`\tau=0.73`$ with $`16\%`$ tied
pairs; boundary ($`\geq 6.0`$) disagreement is $`16.7\%`$. At the frozen
thresholds the heuristic withholds the degradation flag and triggers
calibration; post-hoc inspection agrees on both counts. This is the
second blind evaluation of the frozen thresholds on a pair that
contributed nothing to their selection, and the first on a non-OpenAI
provider. The same scope caveats as F1 apply: served weights are not
frozen, decoding is provider-default, and the estimates are descriptive.

## Appendix K Post-hoc structural audits (amendments G1–G3, G5, G7)

Five exploratory analyses, registered post hoc as amendments. G1–G3 and
G5 reuse only frozen calls; G7 adds 432 new calls under the frozen
stage-2 protocol to test two G1 channels causally. None of the endpoints
below is confirmatory.

Residual structure (amendment G1). The main text attributes the
post-clamp residual to an object-dependent response-function change;
this audit asks whether that residual has observable structure. Using
the F3 second pool pass as an independent replicate, the per-object
version gap’s split-half reliability is $`r=0.25`$ (Qwen), $`0.81`$
(Gemma), and $`0.33`$ (Mistral, where old-judge noise dominates).
Regressing the single-pass gap on object features (year, subfield,
reviewer scores, risk-factor count, log length, verified recognition;
OLS with HC3 errors and a 2,000-draw permutation test on $`R^{2}`$): on
Gemma, where the gap is reliable, features explain $`R^{2}=0.46`$
(permutation $`p<5\times 10^{-4}`$), about $`56\%`$ of the reliability
ceiling: the newer judge scores longer ($`t=-3.7`$), higher-rated-rigor
($`t=-2.6`$), and previously recognized ($`t=-3.2`$) proposals
relatively more favorably, directionally consistent with its sharpened
discrimination and its higher external validity (below). On Qwen the gap
itself is only weakly reliable and features do not predict it
($`R^{2}=0.13`$, permutation $`p=0.13`$). On Mistral the regression
describes the _old_ instrument, since the new one is degenerate, and is
dominated by length ($`R^{2}=0.27`$, about 80% of ceiling). The residual
is therefore not unstructured noise: where it is measurable it is
substantially attribute-driven. This sharpens the response-function
interpretation without claiming mechanism identification.

Cross-family replacement (amendment G2). All six frozen judges scored
the same 220-object pool at L0, so every cross-family pair can be read
descriptively as a judge replacement. Within-family upgrades (the
paper’s three transitions) give $`\tau=0.19`$–$`0.61`$ and top-30% flips
of $`34`$–$`52\%`$ (excess $`+12`$ to $`+35`$ points over the F3
same-version noise floor). The twelve cross-family pairs give location
gaps up to $`1.6`$ pooled SD, $`\tau=0.18`$–$`0.56`$ (median $`0.30`$),
flips of $`40`$–$`77\%`$ (excess $`+20`$ to $`+64`$), and
accept-boundary disagreement up to $`59\%`$. The most practice-like
swap, a current-generation cross-provider replacement (J_current $`\to`$
G_new), moves the location by $`0.82`$ SD and flips $`40\%`$ of the
top-30% set. Cross-family replacement is at least as disruptive as
within-family upgrade on every metric, and usually more. One orthogonal
observation: G_new tracks reviewer soundness best of all six judges
(Spearman $`\rho=0.59`$ vs. $`0.29`$–$`0.35`$), so a replacement can
simultaneously be the best available instrument _and_ a rank-order
break; the audit question and the quality question are separate.

Audit protocol stress tests (amendment G3). Three failure-boundary
questions, answered on the four anchor-scored transitions (three
open-weight, one API), with the frozen diagnosis (degradation flag,
calibration trigger) as ground truth. _Size_: under stratified
subsampling, the full-set diagnosis is reproduced at $`\geq 94\%`$ from
$`n=16`$–$`24`$ anchors on the two healthy open-weight chains. The
collapsed Mistral chain needs $`n\geq 40`$ under the separation flag
alone, because the flag divides by within-group SD, which a degenerate
judge makes vanishingly small and unstable; pairing it with the
degeneracy statistic (modal-score share $`>0.5`$), as the amended audit
already recommends, reaches $`84\%`$ agreement at $`n=12`$ and $`100\%`$
at $`n=32`$. _Sampling policy_: unstratified anchor selection leaves the
health check undefined in $`62\%`$ of draws at $`n=8`$ (no clean–flawed
pairs sampled); stratification is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
_Thresholds_: the degradation flag is stable across the full sweep
($`0.10`$–$`0.60`$) on every chain; the calibration trigger is
threshold-sensitive only where measured disagreement sits near the
$`10\%`$ boundary (the API pair, at $`12.5\%`$: anchor-level subsample
agreement plateaus near $`70\%`$, and only the F2 pool-scale check
resolves the verdict). In practice, about 24 stratified anchors suffice
for healthy instruments; degenerate instruments are caught earlier by
degeneracy statistics than by separation; and near-threshold trigger
verdicts should be escalated to a pool-scale check rather than read off
the anchor audit.

Rationale coding: mapping-shift vs. content-shift (amendment G5). A
location gap could arise from the same assessment mapped to a different
number (mapping-shift) or from a substantively different assessment
(content-shift); scores alone cannot tell them apart. For every pool
object we coded the paired old/new L0 rationales with a frozen
third-party coder (DeepSeek v4-flash; fields: criteria cited, defects
cited, overall stance, and a same-substance judgment; $`188`$–$`217`$
coded pairs per chain). Coding validity: the coded stance change tracks
the actual score change at Spearman $`\rho=0.62`$–$`0.69`$ on all three
chains. Among objects whose score moved by $`\geq 1`$ point, the pure
mapping-shift share is $`46\%`$ (Qwen), $`19\%`$ (Gemma), and $`16\%`$
(Mistral): on two of three chains, most score movement co-occurs with
_changed cited defects_, not a re-scored identical assessment.
Directions match the chain narratives: Gemma’s new judge cites $`0.94`$
fewer defects per object (consistent with its lenient-ward shift and
sharpened discrimination), while Qwen’s new judge cites $`0.77`$ _more_
defects yet lands more positive stances: severity language and score
mapping move independently. Caveats: a single LLM coder without human
calibration; construct validity rests on the stance–score correlation
above; exploratory, not preregistered.

Targeted counterfactual perturbation (amendment G7; design and endpoints
frozen before any edit or call). G1 identifies length and stylistic
features as the strongest observable correlates of the Gemma per-object
gap; this amendment asks whether two of them _mediate_ it. The 24 pool
objects with the largest absolute gap deviation were re-scored by both
Gemma judges (three repeats, stage-2 decoding) under three arms: the
original text (identity), a neutral-editor compression to $`\sim`$60%
length preserving every technical claim and number (median realized
ratio $`0.69`$; median number-preservation $`1.00`$), and a
sentence-level rewrite removing hedging and rhetorical emphasis at
constant length ($`0.98`$). The frozen validity gate passes
emphatically: the identity-arm gap reproduces the original pool gap at
$`r=0.93`$: the per-object gap is a stable property of the object, not
session noise. Both causal contrasts are null: compression changes the
absolute gap by $`+0.02`$ SD $`[-0.15,+0.21]`$ and neutralization by
$`+0.04`$ $`[-0.06,+0.15]`$; movement toward the global mean gap is
likewise null. The G1 features therefore predict _which_ objects move,
but the two manipulable surface attributes are not the mediators;
effects beyond about $`0.2`$ SD are excluded. The response-function
change binds to content properties that survive compression and
de-hedging. One logged patch: the frozen editor exhausted its API
balance mid-generation, so all edits were regenerated with a single
local neutral editor, phi-4, the stage-0b fact extractor; no judge call
preceded the substitution.

## Appendix L A second domain: code review (amendment G6)

Design (frozen before any call). Do the instrument-level phenomena of
location shift, discrimination change, and rank instability appear
outside proposal-style text? We built 48 Python code-review objects from
HumanEvalPack (Muennighoff et al., 2024): 24 canonical solutions (clean)
and 24 with an injected, documented bug (flawed). All six frozen
open-weight judges scored them under an L0-style code-review rubric with
the same JSON-final-line protocol, three repeats per object per judge
(864 calls), stage-0a decoding and per-call seeds. The endpoints (the
per-chain trio) and a no-pass/fail scope declaration were frozen in
amendment_G6.json before any call.

Results. On Qwen, $`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=-0.08`$ SD $`[-0.17,+0.01]`$:
no detectable location shift on code, against a robust lenient-ward
shift on proposals; separation stays high on both versions
($`4.9\to 4.6`$); $`\tau=0.88`$. On Gemma,
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=+0.19`$ $`[-0.04,+0.42]`$, with a CI crossing
zero and a strictward point estimate against the lenient-ward proposal
shift, but the discrimination sharpening replicates: separation
$`1.61\to 4.64`$; $`\tau=0.67`$. On Mistral,
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{ver}}=-0.79`$ $`[-0.95,-0.64]`$, a large lenient-ward
shift with means $`5.04\to 6.83`$, and _no collapse_: separation
$`1.66\to 1.50`$, modal-score share $`31\%`$, against $`93\%`$ on the
proposal pool. Caveats include that old Qwen truncated 29/144 calls at
the 4,096-token budget, so per-object medians use the surviving repeats
and 46/48 objects are retained; the other five judges had at most 7
failures. No same-version repeated-call baseline was run on this domain,
and a single rubric was used.

The phenomena are therefore not proposal-specific, but their sign and
magnitude are domain-specific. The weight pair that degenerates on
proposal scoring is a functioning, if strongly shifted, instrument on
code review; the chain with the most robust proposal shift is the
flattest here even as its discrimination sharpening carries over. A
version transition cannot be characterized once and applied everywhere.
An anchor audit is informative about the domain its anchors are drawn
from, and should be re-run per deployment domain.

## Appendix M The response-function model: proposition, proof, and scope

This appendix states and proves the claim used as an interpretive device
in Section 3 and Section 7: within the model, a global monotone
recalibration of old scores into new scores exists when the criterion
weights are collinear. The main text uses only the observable
signatures. We do not claim that the weights themselves are identified.

Setup. Each judge version $`v`$ scores an object $`x`$ as

```math
s_{v}(x)=g_{v}\!\left(w_{v}^{\top}z(x)+b_{v}\right),
```

where $`z(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{K}`$ collects latent criterion-level
assessments of $`x`$, $`w_{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}\setminus\{0\}`$ weights
them, $`b_{v}\in\mathbb{R}`$, and $`g_{v}`$ is strictly increasing.
Write $`o`$ and $`n`$ for the old and new versions and
$`Z=\{z(x):x\in\mathcal{X}\}`$ for the realized assessment set. We call
$`Z`$ _rich_ if the difference set $`Z-Z`$ meets every nonempty open
cone in $`\mathbb{R}^{K}`$; it suffices, for instance, that $`Z`$
contain an open ball.

###### Proposition 1 (global recalibration requires preserved geometry).

Assume $`Z`$ is rich. (i) If $`w_{n}=c\,w_{o}`$ for some $`c>0`$, then
$`s_{n}=h\circ s_{o}`$ on all of $`\mathcal{X}`$ for the strictly
increasing map
$`h(s)=g_{n}\!\left(c\left(g_{o}^{-1}(s)-b_{o}\right)+b_{n}\right)`$.
(ii) If $`w_{n}`$ is not a scalar multiple of $`w_{o}`$, then there
exist pairs whose order the two versions agree on and pairs whose order
they reverse; consequently no monotone $`h`$, increasing or decreasing,
satisfies $`s_{n}=h\circ s_{o}`$ on $`\mathcal{X}`$, and every monotone
recalibration of old scores mis-ranks some pair relative to the new
judge. (iii) The remaining case, $`w_{n}=c\,w_{o}`$ with $`c<0`$, admits
the strictly decreasing $`h`$ of the same form: the versions agree as
instruments only up to a global inversion of the scale.

###### Proof.

(i) and (iii) are the displayed composition:
$`g_{o}^{-1}(s_{o}(x))-b_{o}=w_{o}^{\top}z(x)`$, so
$`h(s_{o}(x))=g_{n}(c\,w_{o}^{\top}z(x)+b_{n})=s_{n}(x)`$; $`h`$ is
strictly increasing for $`c>0`$ and strictly decreasing for $`c<0`$.
(ii) Suppose $`w_{n}`$ is not a scalar multiple of $`w_{o}`$. Both cones
$`C_{\pm}=\{u:w_{o}^{\top}u>0,\ \pm\,w_{n}^{\top}u>0\}`$ are nonempty
and open: since $`w_{n}\not\parallel w_{o}`$ there is a $`u_{\perp}`$
with $`w_{o}^{\top}u_{\perp}=0`$ and $`w_{n}^{\top}u_{\perp}>0`$, and
for any $`u_{0}`$ with $`w_{o}^{\top}u_{0}>0`$ the vector
$`u_{0}+t\,u_{\perp}`$ lies in $`C_{+}`$ and $`u_{0}-t\,u_{\perp}`$ in
$`C_{-}`$ for $`t`$ large. Richness supplies $`z_{i}-z_{j}\in C_{+}`$
and $`z_{k}-z_{l}\in C_{-}`$. By strict monotonicity of $`g_{o},g_{n}`$,
the first pair satisfies $`s_{o}(x_{i})>s_{o}(x_{j})`$ and
$`s_{n}(x_{i})>s_{n}(x_{j})`$ (order preserved), the second
$`s_{o}(x_{k})>s_{o}(x_{l})`$ and $`s_{n}(x_{k})<s_{n}(x_{l})`$ (order
reversed). An increasing $`h`$ fails on the second pair, a decreasing
$`h`$ on the first, and any monotone recalibration mis-ranks whichever
pair contradicts its direction. ∎

###### Corollary 1 (what a global map can and cannot absorb).

If $`g_{o}=g_{n}=\mathrm{id}`$, the version gap is affine in the
assessments:
$`\Delta(x)=s_{o}(x)-s_{n}(x)=(w_{o}-w_{n})^{\top}z(x)+(b_{o}-b_{n})`$.
It is constant across objects iff $`w_{o}=w_{n}`$. An affine correction
of old scores removes the constant part and the component of
$`w_{o}-w_{n}`$ along $`w_{o}`$; any orthogonal component leaves
object-level residual dispersion that no function of $`s_{o}`$ can
absorb, because objects with equal $`s_{o}`$ carry different $`\Delta`$.

Empirical correspondences. The model’s three degrees of freedom map onto
the paper’s measurements as follows. _Origin:_ the mean gap is
repairable by fitting on representative objects (pool-refit explained
fraction $`0.76`$–$`0.81`$ on Qwen; Table 11), as any $`b`$-change is.
_Geometry:_ the sub-$`8\%`$ dispersion absorption (Appendix F.2), the
tertile sign reversal (Gemma $`+0.80`$ vs. $`-0.43`$ SD; Appendix F.6),
and the excess top-30% flips beyond the same-version noise floor
($`+12`$–$`35`$ points; Appendix J) are the three signatures Corollary 1
and Proposition 1(ii) predict for $`w_{n}\not\parallel w_{o}`$ and rule
out for a pure origin change. _Resolution:_ a near-flat $`g_{n}`$ over
the realized range produces near-constant scores regardless of
$`w_{n}^{\top}z`$, while upstream channels remain intact, which is the
measured Mistral 3.2 pattern (object-specific rationales, discriminative
defect counts, saturated scalar map; Appendix F.1); under collapse,
recalibrating $`s_{o}`$ is vacuous and the instrument fails outright.

Scope. Three idealizations bound the reading. First, $`z(x)`$ is latent:
the data identify the class of change through the signatures above, not
the weights themselves; estimating $`\Delta w`$ requires interventions
that move one criterion at a time with matched placebo edits, which our
design does not include. Second, linearity is assumed; a nonlinear
aggregator changes the algebra but not the moral, since the proof only
needs two objects whose relative order the versions disagree on. Third,
$`z(x)`$ is taken version-invariant; the rationale coding of Appendix K
suggests the versions partly _read_ objects differently (content-shift
majorities on two chains), a version-dependent $`z_{v}(x)`$ that global
scalar maps can represent even less. The G7 nulls (compression and
de-hedging leave the gap unchanged) narrow the changed directions to
content properties that survive both edits, consistent with
criterion-level rather than surface-level movement.
